The solution

Eleven models. One irrigation decision.

Every recommendation you see on a farmer's phone is the output of a chain of prediction models, each trained on sensor data, weather history and plot-level records.

Prediction models

What the system predicts

These models run continuously in the background, re-scoring every plot as new sensor and weather data arrives.

SCHEDULING

Next irrigation date

Predicts the optimal date to irrigate each plot based on current soil and crop status.

SCHEDULING

Irrigation duration

Recommends how long the pump should run to reach target soil moisture without overshoot.

WATER

Crop water requirement

Estimates how much water the crop needs at its current growth stage.

RISK

Water stress probability

Flags plots at rising risk of moisture stress before visible symptoms appear.

WEATHER

Rainfall-adjusted recommendation

Holds back irrigation advisories automatically when meaningful rainfall is forecast.

RISK

Yield loss from delay

Estimates the yield cost of postponing irrigation by a given number of days.

OPERATIONS

Pump scheduling optimisation

Aligns irrigation timing with electricity availability windows to cut wasted power.

NUTRITION

Fertigation recommendation

Suggests fertigation dose and timing alongside irrigation, using soil nutrient data.

RISK

Disease and stress forecasting

Surfaces early warning signals for disease pressure linked to moisture conditions.

YIELD

Yield prediction

Projects expected and potential yield range per plot ahead of harvest.

LLM

Farmer-friendly advisory generation

Converts every prediction above into one short, plain-language message per plot.

Data platform

What feeds the models

The models are only as good as what feeds them. Four sources come together in one agricultural data platform.

IoT soil sensors Weather forecasts Satellite imagery Geofenced plot boundaries Soil test records Farmer profiles Historical yield data Electricity availability windows

Governance, by design

  • Role-based access and encryption for farmer and plot data, aligned with the DPDP Act.
  • Human-in-the-loop review — agronomists can override any AI recommendation.
  • Model monitoring and periodic validation before any recommendation reaches a farmer.
